# Script review · The Day the Letter Came

The full dialogue in `dist/story.js` and the embedded scene text in `dist/game.js` were reviewed for continuity, natural English subtitle wording, pacing and emotional weight. The pass kept the letter and final conversation intact while shortening explanatory captions and correcting lines that contradicted the playable scenes.

## Continuity and wording changes

- Qiang remains the opposing goalie. His line after Mother's call now says “Come on. We’re still playing,” rather than putting Sheng on Qiang's side.
- Mother takes a message for her notes now and says they will write after dinner. Sheng returns to the yard briefly before rain starts, giving “Go on. Before it gets dark” an ordinary afternoon around it.
- The child and young adult remain the same Sheng. The Hong Kong caption states the year and age plainly; the relative's “our address” establishes practical family help without inventing a migration route. A duplicate explanatory caption was removed, and the caption's line break was corrected.
- The ladder is a simple climb within an explicitly labelled study daydream. “Is it steady?” replaces a motivational slogan. Sheng asks his classmate to hold it, and the upper climb opens after that visible help.
- Sheng considers what to move before the player chooses the practice order. The classmate refers to tomorrow's late shift; a “The next evening” transition introduces a different study partner. The late classmate then arrives at eight and receives an explanation of the diagram they saved for him.
- The Shanghai colleague's farewell no longer places the friend downstairs. The short crowd crossing leads to the friend by the river, where Jo says she likes being there. Shanghai and the subsequent local Ningbo drive remain distinct scenes.
- Optional road photos receive a short arrival exchange: the friend will send what they took, and Jo will show Auntie. An empty album receives no reprimand or compensating speech.
- Jo inspects “things,” rather than “remembered places.” The courtyard is familiar to the player; Jo recognises her father's photograph. Notebook dialogue uses the ordinary word “mending,” and the household record makes Mother's income part of the ball repair.
- Jo says Grandma recorded what Sheng wanted her to “tell him,” so the introduction fits both the saved question and the two saved statements.

## Beats preserved

The shoe argument, borrowed-book joke and Father's one-kick excuse retain the story's ordinary humour. Adults speak about addresses, meals, work, repairs and study rather than explaining the themes.

The first childhood choice is retained across replay and repeated exactly in the notebook and phone conversation:

- “Do they play football there?”
- “I want to show him my new trick.”
- “Qiang keeps moving the goal.”

The father's original English letter and modern readable Chinese draft remain intact. The money was put aside for a fare home; the letter asks about the roof, shoes and Chunxiu's cough. The notebook holds a retained copy of Mother's outgoing reply, alongside her household records. Her labour, repair arrangements and neighbours' help remain visible.

Sheng's disappointment is not cancelled by discovering the letter. He says he wanted his father home, and explicitly says his father returned later, when Sheng was taller than him. There is no surprise death, verdict against migration or instruction for Jo to leave Shanghai.

The ending remains a conversation and an easy action: Jo shows the yard, returns a present-day child's ball, and hears “Don’t hang up yet.” The optional father chapter gives him workmates and humour; it is not needed to understand the main ending and does not quote a reply he has not yet received.
